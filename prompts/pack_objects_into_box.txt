Completion requirement: All four objects are placed in the aligned box with correct facing direction, and the robot returns to origin.

Packing guidance from the available TRAIN demonstrations:

Plan the reachable workspace before grasping. Choose the arm on the object's side, and consider the forearm's swept path as well as the fingertip target. A clear endpoint does not make a diagonal path through the box safe. Lift clear of obstacles before translating; keep the idle arm out of the box and the other arm's route. Do not copy a demonstration's choice of arm when the current object is on the opposite side.

The demonstrations bring the empty box toward the robots into a shared, reachable working area, after moving obstructing objects aside if needed. They do not push it to the far edge to make room. Relevant examples are pack_objects_into_box-0000:p03 (left arm), -0001:p03 and -0002:p02 (right arm). Keep an already aligned, reachable box in place. If adjustment is needed, inspect the matching box grasp AND release reference: the examples grasp the exposed upper side flap with a tilted wrist, not the low front wall with a fully vertical wrist. Use a short, level move. Compare the empty box with the reference release image: its two tall side flaps end up on the left and right, not front and back. Merely making some edges parallel to the table does not distinguish these two orientations. Verify that the box settles flat and that both arms can enter and withdraw before loading it. Absolute demonstration positions are not current targets. Align the empty box first; rotating a loaded box also rotates its contents.

At a box or object grasp, compare the demonstrated wrist quaternion, measured link6 pose and finger placement with the live wrist image. Preserve a suitable demonstrated wrist pitch during transport instead of assuming every grasp needs an exact vertical wrist. Slow down near rims and contact; split large rotations from translation in clear space. A tracking interruption calls for a small retreat toward the last clear measured pose and a different approach, not repeated pushes toward the same blocked target.

The close selector detects a command crossing 0.5, not physical contact. A broad shoe can be held with a gripper command above 0.5: pack_objects_into_box-0002:p05 has such a grasp. If command_event_found=false, the returned window may be early approach. Inspect section=end for that grasp phase, or section=start of its following carry phase (-0002:p06), to understand the held-object wrist/gripper relationship. Those windows show an already lifted shoe: their height is NOT a table-contact height. Ground contact height and finger placement in the current head/wrist images. Judge grasp success by the object following a small lift; do not force the fingers fully shut merely to match a threshold.

Determine the held object's front from the live image (shoe toe, vehicle front, hammer head, toothbrush head). Track that direction relative to the wrist through the lift, then turn the held object toward the requested left direction in clear space. A copied wrist quaternion alone does not fix object orientation. The demonstrations place elongated objects across the box, lower into available interior space, release, and lift clear before moving sideways. Use the visible interior rather than the outside flaps as the placement region; account for the object's extent on both sides of the grasp and the objects already packed. Avoid dragging an open gripper sideways through the rim or contents after release.

Confirm each object remains inside with its front facing left after the fingers withdraw. If occluded, use the active wrist view before placing the next object. Preserve the upright box and reserve room for the remaining objects. Once all four placements are verified, clear both hands above/outside the box and return home.
